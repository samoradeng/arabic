# Jordanian Cultural Guide

Essential cultural knowledge for anyone learning Jordanian Arabic.

---

## Core Values

### 1. الكرم (il-karam) — Generosity
- The highest virtue in Jordanian society
- Hosts will go above and beyond for guests
- Refusing hospitality is offensive
- If someone pays for your meal, insist once, then accept gracefully

### 2. العيب (il-3eib) — Social Shame
- Unspoken rules about what's "inappropriate"
- Covers behavior, speech, dress, and social interactions
- "3eib!" is said when someone crosses a social line
- Varies by family — some are more conservative than others

### 3. الشرف (ish-sharaf) — Honor
- Family honor is taken very seriously
- Don't speak badly about someone's family
- Compliment people's families when appropriate

### 4. الواسطة (il-waas6a) — Connections
- Using personal connections to get things done
- Deeply embedded in the culture — not seen as corruption
- "Do you know someone who...?" is a normal question

## Social Rules

### Greetings
- **Always greet first.** Not greeting is rude.
- **Shake hands** with the same gender freely.
- **With opposite gender:** Wait for the woman to extend her hand. If she doesn't, just nod and say مرحبا.
- **Elders first.** Greet the oldest person in the room first.
- **Multiple greetings:** It's normal to exchange 3-4 greetings before getting to the point.

### Visiting a Home
1. Remove shoes at the door (unless told otherwise)
2. Bring a gift — sweets, chocolate, or fruit
3. Accept all food and drink offered (refuse once politely, then accept)
4. Compliment the house and the food
5. Don't leave immediately after eating — stay for tea
6. Don't open closed doors or wander without invitation

### Eating
- Eat with your **right hand** (left hand is considered unclean)
- Say **بسم الله** (bismillah) before starting
- Say **الحمد لله** (il-7amdu lillah) when finished
- Compliment the cook: **تسلم إيديك**
- If eating mansaf traditionally, eat from the communal plate
- Don't waste food — take what you'll eat

### Gift Giving
- Gifts should be wrapped nicely
- Don't give alcohol (unless you know they drink)
- Sweets are always safe
- Flowers are fine for hosts
- Don't give an odd number of flowers (associated with funerals in some cultures)

## Religious Awareness

### Islam in Daily Life
Jordan is predominantly Muslim. These affect daily life:
- **Five daily prayers** — businesses may pause briefly
- **Friday** is the holy day (like Sunday in Christianity)
- **Ramadan** — one month of fasting sunrise to sunset
  - Don't eat/drink in public during fasting hours (out of respect)
  - Greet with رمضان كريم (rama9aan kareem)
  - Evenings are festive — people eat together (إفطار iftar)
- **Mosques** — you'll hear the أذان (adhaan — call to prayer) 5 times daily

### Christian Minority
- About 6% of Jordan is Christian
- Christmas and Easter are public holidays
- Christians and Muslims coexist peacefully
- Churches are common in Amman, Madaba, Salt

### Key Religious Phrases Everyone Uses
These are cultural, not just religious — even non-religious people say them:

| Phrase | When to Use |
|--------|-------------|
| إن شاء الله (inshaallah) | Any future plan |
| الحمد لله (il-7amdu lillah) | Gratitude, when asked how you are |
| ما شاء الله (mashaallah) | Admiring something (prevents evil eye) |
| بسم الله (bismillah) | Before starting anything |
| الله يرحمه (allah yir7amu) | When someone has passed away |
| الله يشفيه (allah yishfeeh) | When someone is sick |
| لا حول ولا قوة إلا بالله | Shock, frustration, difficult news |

## Topics to Avoid

1. **Criticizing the King** — This is illegal in Jordan. Don't do it.
2. **Palestine/Israel** — Sensitive topic. Listen first, form opinions carefully.
3. **Questioning someone's religion** — Off limits.
4. **Sex and relationships** — Very private. Don't ask.
5. **Insulting someone's family** — Never, ever.
6. **Comparing Jordan unfavorably** — Be diplomatic.

## Safe Topics

1. **Food** — Everyone loves talking about food
2. **Family** — Ask about their kids, parents, etc.
3. **Football (soccer)** — Jordanians are passionate fans
4. **Tourism in Jordan** — Petra, Wadi Rum, Dead Sea, Aqaba
5. **Arabic language** — People love that you're learning
6. **Weather** — Universal small talk
7. **Jordanian hospitality** — Compliment it; they're proud of it

## Money & Tipping

- Currency: Jordanian Dinar (JOD / دينار)
- 1 Dinar = 100 piasters (قرش 2irsh)
- 1 JOD ≈ 1.41 USD (relatively strong currency)
- **Tipping:** 10% at restaurants, round up for taxis
- **Bargaining:** Expected at souks, NOT at malls/supermarkets
