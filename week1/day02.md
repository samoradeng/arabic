# Day 2: Family, Descriptions & "To Be"

## Lesson Goals
- Describe people and things
- Talk about your family
- Understand that Jordanian Arabic has no "to be" verb in present tense
- Use possessive suffixes

---

## 1. Family Members — العيلة

Family is *everything* in Jordan. You'll be asked about yours constantly.

| Arabic | Transliteration | Meaning |
|--------|----------------|---------|
| عيلة | 3eile | family |
| أب / بابا | ab / baaba | father / dad |
| أم / ماما | umm / maama | mother / mom |
| أخ | akh | brother |
| أخت | ukht | sister |
| إبن | ibin | son |
| بنت | bint | daughter / girl |
| جد / سيدي | jidd / seedi | grandfather |
| جدة / ستي | jidde / sitti | grandmother |
| عم | 3amm | paternal uncle |
| خال | khaal | maternal uncle |
| عمة | 3amme | paternal aunt |
| خالة | khaale | maternal aunt |
| إبن عم | ibin 3amm | cousin (paternal, m) |
| بنت خال | bint khaal | cousin (maternal, f) |
| زوج | zooj | husband |
| زوجة | zooje | wife |
| ولاد | wlaad | children / kids |

> **Cultural Note:** Jordanians distinguish between paternal relatives (عم/عمة) and maternal relatives (خال/خالة). This distinction matters socially.

## 2. "To Be" — There Isn't One

In Jordanian Arabic, **there is no verb "to be" in present tense.** You just put the subject and the predicate together:

| Arabic | Transliteration | Literal | Meaning |
|--------|----------------|---------|---------|
| أنا تعبان | ana ta3baan | I tired | I am tired |
| هو طويل | huwwe 6aweel | He tall | He is tall |
| هي حلوة | hiyye 7ilwe | She pretty | She is pretty |
| البيت كبير | il-beit kbeer | The house big | The house is big |
| الأكل زاكي | il-akil zaaki | The food delicious | The food is delicious |

This is one of the easiest parts of Arabic — just skip "is/am/are."

## 3. Adjectives — الصفات

Adjectives come **after** the noun and must match in gender:

**Masculine → Feminine: usually add ـة (-e)**

| Masculine | Feminine | Transliteration | Meaning |
|-----------|----------|----------------|---------|
| كبير | كبيرة | kbeer / kbeere | big |
| صغير | صغيرة | z-gheer / z-gheere | small |
| طويل | طويلة | 6aweel / 6aweele | tall / long |
| قصير | قصيرة | 2a9eer / 2a9eere | short |
| حلو | حلوة | 7ilu / 7ilwe | sweet / pretty |
| زاكي | زاكية | zaaki / zaakye | delicious |
| منيح | منيحة | mnee7 / mnee7a | good |
| مش منيح | مش منيحة | mish mnee7 / mnee7a | not good |
| جديد | جديدة | jdeed / jdeede | new |
| قديم | قديمة | 2adeem / 2adeeme | old (things) |
| كبير بالعمر | كبيرة بالعمر | kbeer bil-3umur | old (people) |
| شاطر | شاطرة | shaa6ir / shaa6ra | clever / hard-working |
| هادي | هادية | haadi / haadye | calm / quiet |

> **Jordanian Tip:** منيح (*mnee7*) is THE Jordanian word for "good." In Syrian Arabic it's منيح too, but in Lebanese you might hear "marii2." If you say منيح, people know you speak Jordanian.

## 4. Possessive Suffixes — ضمائر الملكية

Instead of separate possessive words, Arabic attaches suffixes to nouns:

| Suffix | Transliteration | Meaning | Example: بيت (beit = house) |
|--------|----------------|---------|----------------------------|
| ـي | -i | my | بيتي — beiti (my house) |
| ـك | -ak (m) / -ik (f) | your | بيتك — beitak (your house) |
| ـه | -uh | his | بيته — beituh (his house) |
| ـها | -ha | her | بيتها — beitha (her house) |
| ـنا | -na | our | بيتنا — beitna (our house) |
| ـكم | -kum | your (pl) | بيتكم — beitkum (your house) |
| ـهم | -hum | their | بيتهم — beithum (their house) |

### Practice: Attach possessives to these words

- أخ (akh — brother): أخوي akhuui (my brother), أخوك akhuuk (your brother)...
- أم (umm — mother): أمي ummi, أمك ummak...
- اسم (isim — name): اسمي ismi, اسمك ismak...

> **Note:** Some nouns slightly change form when you add suffixes. أخ → أخو- is a common one.

## 5. Describing Family — Dialogue

> — عندك إخوان؟
> — 3indak ikhwaan?
> — *Do you have siblings?*

> — آه، عندي أخ واحد وأختين.
> — aah, 3indi akh waa7ad w ukhteen.
> — *Yes, I have one brother and two sisters.*

> — ما شاء الله. أخوك كبير ولا صغير؟
> — mashaallah. akhuuk kbeer willa z-gheer?
> — *Mashallah. Is your brother older or younger?*

> — أخوي أكبر مني. هو متزوج وعنده ولدين.
> — akhuui akbar minni. huwwe mitzawwij w 3induh waladein.
> — *My brother is older than me. He's married and has two kids.*

> — وأخواتك؟
> — w akhawaatik?
> — *And your sisters?*

> — وحدة بتدرس بالجامعة والتانية بالمدرسة.
> — wa7de bitidrus bil-jaam3a w it-taanye bil-madrase.
> — *One studies at university and the other is in school.*
